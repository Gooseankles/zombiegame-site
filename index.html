Zombie Dawn

An open-world survival game for a group of friends, on a cold northern coast. Get out of the town, find somewhere quiet to build, stay warm and dry, and in the end clear the zombies and bring the world back.

Pre-alpha: placeholder art, missing pieces, and things that break between versions. Working title.

Download for Windows Looking for the newest version…

Unzip it anywhere and run ZombieDawn.exe. Start Steam first to play with friends.

When a new version is out, the game says so on its start screen and updates itself with one button.

Story

The Walking Winter

The opening of a story from the island. The game lets you find the rest yourself.

I woke up on a road with gravel in my cheek and a sound in my head like a kettle that would not stop.

The last thing I remembered was the harbour at Vellport. A man in a hi-vis vest shouting at a ferry that was already gone. A woman holding a child so tightly her knuckles were white, and the child looking over her shoulder at something behind me. Then the noise, and the sky turning sideways.

A sign said HALDERSFIELD 2. Someone had left a car across the lane with its door open. The radio inside was still talking in the flat voice they use for numbers. All crossings to the mainland are closed until further notice. Do not approach the water. Do not try to leave the island.

It was full morning, and along the village street the lamps were still burning. Nobody had come to switch them off.

Someone had painted on the wall by the village sign, in a hurry:

To find a cure you need to search where it all began.

Under it, in another hand: Don’t trust the city. Don’t trust the radio. A few steps on, an official notice, stamped, promised that the city across the water was safe, and told everyone to report to a harbour there.

I looked up. Past the end of the island, over the water, was the city. A long grey line of towers, very clear, a few lights still in the windows. Between us and it a thin black thread went out over the sea and ended in nothing. Somebody had blown the bridge.

I took a knife from the car’s glovebox, bound my head with a rag, and went down into the village.

The first three days I did what anyone does. I found a house with a stove and a woodpile. I learned that the river water would make me sick, and that if I boiled it, it would not. I learned that the dead in Haldersfield did not stay dead, and that they were drawn to noise, to fire, and to the smell of a living thing. I learned to move at the edge of a street, to leave doors closed, to hold my breath when one passed.

I found a kitchen table set for breakfast, with nobody in the house. I found a delivery van with its rear doors open and a garden hose still running into the road.

The rest of the story is in the game.

The goal: find and shut down the source of the outbreak, clear the zombies zone by zone, get power, water and the radio running again, and call in the ship that ends the game.
